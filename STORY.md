# NEON ECHO — The Story of James & Millie

> *"Count the stars with me. I'll always be one of them."*
> — Mara Vance, to her children

A 2D cyberpunk story told across **five continuous episodes**, each one opening on
the exact beat the last one cut away from, and each one ending on a knife-edge.
You play **James Vance**, hunting the drowned neon sprawl of **Verge City** for his
younger sister **Millie** — and the deeper he digs, the more he learns that she is
not the person he is trying to rescue. In the **flashbacks** you play as Millie,
and those bright, warm memories are where the truth of the story is quietly buried.

The narrative is **not** delivered only as title cards between chapters. It is told
*inside the levels* — as floating **subtitles** while you run, **pop-up dialogue**
when characters speak over the comm, and face-to-face **meetings** where the people
of Verge City (and Millie herself) step into the same frame as you.

---

## Craft notes — what this is built on

This script was written against the spine of stories that do the
"someone-I-love-is-out-there" premise best, and tries to honour their lessons:

| Influence | What it taught this story |
| --- | --- |
| *The Last of Us* | A bond is the plot. The world is just pressure on it. |
| *Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons* | Let the player **be** both siblings; grief lands harder when your own hands held the other character. |
| *Taken* | Relentless forward motion; every scene is a door kicked in. |
| *Blade Runner* / *Cyberpunk 2077* | The city is cruel by design; the corporation is the monster. |
| *Children of Men* | Bleak world, one fragile, stubborn ember of hope. |
| *Léon: The Professional* | The protector and the one who turns out not to need protecting. |
| *Life is Strange* | Memory as structure — flashbacks that re-frame the present. |
| *Red Dead Redemption 2* | Loyalty can be a slow tragedy; let the cost accumulate. |
| *God of War (2018)* | Restraint is a character trait; violence should cost something. |
| *Prisoners* | A search will ask you to become someone you don't like. |

**Principles applied:** a protagonist whose *want* (find Millie) is at war with his
*need* (forgive himself, and let her be her own person); an inciting crane; rising
try/fail cycles; a **midpoint reversal** (Episode 3 — she was never a victim); an
**all-is-lost** low (Episode 4); a climax that pays off every seed; and three
running motifs planted early and harvested late — **paper cranes**, the **lullaby
about counting stars**, and the **scar on James's hand**.

## Cast

| Name | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **James Vance** | 28. Protagonist (present day). Ex-courier and one-time low-level Mire enforcer trying to go straight. Half-raised Millie after their parents died. Carries the guilt of the life he led. Playable every present-day chapter. |
| **Millie Vance** | 22. A gifted coder and street-medic; the idealist James protected. Playable in every flashback. Folds paper cranes when she's thinking. |
| **"Books" Okonkwo** | An ageing info-broker in the Undercity who knew the Vance parents. The closest thing James has to family left. |
| **Cray** | Saint-Cloud's lead enforcer. Smiling, patient, cruel. The hand that does Mother's work. |
| **"Mother" / Saint** | The head of **Saint-Cloud Genetics**. Speaks softly. Believes the poor of Verge City are a *resource*. |
| **Mara & Eli Vance** | The siblings' parents (in memory). The lullaby is Mara's; the cranes are her craft. |

## The city — Verge City

A vertical megacity that flooded its own foundations and never looked down again.
Five descending/ascending strata frame the five episodes:

1. **The Sink** — the Undercity slums; tenements and rooftops over black water.
2. **The Loop** — the elevated neon ring-road strangling the mid-city.
3. **The Floodworks** — the drowned lower district; a half-sunken cathedral lab.
4. **The Foundry** — Saint-Cloud's industrial spine; refineries and processing.
5. **The Apex** — Saint-Cloud's crown spire. The only place in Verge City you can
   still see the real sky.

## Backstory

Mara and Eli Vance died when James was eighteen and Millie was twelve — a
"transit collapse" in the Sink that the city ruled an accident. James kept Millie
fed by running packages, then debts, then worse, for the **Mire** syndicate.
Millie grew up brilliant and angry, taught herself code and medicine, and started
asking the question James never dared: *who really owns the dead of the Undercity?*
Six months ago she vanished. Last week, a paper crane arrived at James's door with
her handwriting inside: **"Don't look for me. — M."**

He's looking.

# EPISODE 1 — "Breadcrumbs"
**Stratum:** The Sink · **Play:** James, then young Millie (flashback)
**Mechanics combined:** Running · Parkour · Climbing · Shooting

### Cold open (in-game)
James opens the crane on his fire-escape. Subtitle, his own thought: *"Six months.
And now a paper bird."* A second crane is already gone — a street-kid courier is
running with the data-chip that was folded inside. James gives chase.

### The level (told as you play)
A long descent-and-climb through the Sink: sprint and **vault** the rooftops,
**wall-run** the gaps, **climb** a collapsing tenement, and **fight** the Mire
thugs who recognise their old runner. Pop-up comm dialogue threads through it —
**Books** raises James on a salvaged earpiece and guides him, needling him about
old debts. Halfway, you **meet Books in person** in his junk-shop (a real stop in
the level): he tells James the courier is heading for the Floodworks, and lands the
first hook — *"Millie's not who you remember, son. She walked away from you on
purpose."*

### Flashback — "The Crane" (play as Millie)
Warm magenta wash. *Sixteen years ago.* On a Sink rooftop, twelve-year-old Millie
chases James through the washing-lines, then sits him down and folds his clumsy
hands around a square of paper until it becomes a crane. Mara's lullaby drifts up
from the window. Millie: *"If we ever get lost, leave a trail of these. I'll always
find the next one."* (Sets up the breadcrumb motif and the player's first hands-on
moment as Millie.)

### Cliffhanger
James corners the courier on a rooftop — it's a terrified kid. *"She paid me to slow
you down,"* the kid blurts. A red dot finds the kid's chest; a Mire sniper fires
once. The roof gives way under the impact and the firefight; James drops through
three floors of rotten tenement into the black water of the Sink as the building
folds in on itself. **Cut to black.** *Get to the Floodworks.*

# EPISODE 2 — "The Loop"
**Stratum:** The Loop · **Play:** James, then teen Millie (flashback)
**Mechanics combined:** Driving · Shooting · Parkour (on-foot section)

### Open (in-game)
James drags himself out of the water onto a Loop on-ramp, soaked and bleeding.
Books, over comm: *"Only way across to the Floodworks is the Loop, and Cray's people
own it tonight. Find a car. Don't stop."* He hot-wires an abandoned courier hauler.

### The level
A high-speed run along the neon ring-road. The car now **leans into the climbs and
the downhills** — handle the hills, weave traffic, and **shoot** the Saint-Cloud
interceptors off your tail. Mid-chapter your hauler is rammed into the barrier; you
**bail out and parkour** across a wreck-jam and a seized toll-gate on foot, fighting
through, then drop to the descent. Threaded comm dialogue the whole way; a second,
**distorted voice** breaks onto the channel — clipped, familiar — and goes silent.

### Flashback — "First Light" (play as Millie)
*Six years ago.* A borrowed car on the empty old Loop at 3 a.m.; teenage Millie
behind the wheel, James white-knuckled in the passenger seat, both of them laughing.
She pulls over at the overlook and shows him the thing she built — a little program
that lights up every camera in the district at once, just to prove the city *can* be
seen. The seed of the Ghost.

### Cliffhanger
At the Floodworks descent the distorted channel clears — and it is unmistakably
**Millie**: *"James. Stop. If you come down here, you undo everything I've bled for.
Please — go home."* The line dies. A charge blows the ramp out from under the
descent; James and the wreck of the gate plunge toward the flooded dark. **Cut to
black.** *She's alive. And she doesn't want to be found.*

# EPISODE 3 — "Drowned Light" — *the reversal*
**Stratum:** The Floodworks · **Play:** James, then young Millie (flashback)
**Mechanics combined:** Swimming · Climbing · Shooting · Running

### Open (in-game)
James surfaces in a drowned avenue, neon signs guttering underwater. The
Floodworks: streets become canals, a cathedral half-swallowed by the flood.

### The level
**Swim** the submerged streets (breath matters; surface to breathe), **climb** the
cathedral's drowned rigging out of the water, **fight** Mire divers, and **run** the
flooded nave. Subtitles and comm carry the dread. At the top you reach what you
think is Millie's prison — and it is the opposite: a **command post**. Maps, a wall
of Saint-Cloud's crimes, and **paper cranes strung like prayer flags.** She isn't a
captive here. She's the **Ghost** the whole city whispers about.

### The meeting (in-game, face to face)
Millie steps out of the dark — older, harder, a med-rig on her arm. The siblings are
finally in the same frame. It is not a rescue; it's an argument. She tells him the
"transit collapse" that killed their parents was Saint-Cloud clearing a harvest
site; that the corporation strips bio-data from the Undercity's dead and dying; that
she has spent six months *inside* their system and is days from burning it all down.
*"I kept you out of it to keep you alive. Go home, James."*

### Flashback — "The Question" (play as Millie)
*The night of the collapse.* Play young Millie searching the rubble for her parents,
finding instead a Saint-Cloud salvage tag where the bodies should be. The exact
moment the idealist is born — and the vow.

### Cliffhanger
Cray's strike team breaches the command post — **they followed James to her.** His
search is the thing that finds her. Millie is taken; she doesn't fight, because
fighting would expose what she's hidden in their network. The last thing she does is
press a paper crane into James's hand before the divers drag her into the flood.
**Cut to black.** *You led them straight to her.*

# EPISODE 4 — "The Foundry" — *all is lost*
**Stratum:** The Foundry · **Play:** James, then Millie (flashback)
**Mechanics combined:** Riding (motorbike) · Shooting · Climbing · Running

### Open (in-game)
Guilt is the engine now. Books, quiet on the comm: *"This isn't your fault, James."*
James: *"It's exactly my fault."* He takes a courier bike into the Foundry, toward
Saint-Cloud's processing plant.

### The level
**Ride** the industrial arteries, **shooting** the riders Cray throws at you; ditch
the bike and **climb** the refinery rigging; **run and fight** the catwalks into the
plant. Inside, the horror is plain — the "processing" of the Undercity's people into
product. You **meet Cray**, who is in no hurry, and who tells you the worst thing: you
were never the rescuer. *"Mother wanted you found so the Ghost would surface to save
you. You're the bait, James. You've always been the bait."*

### Flashback — "The Bargain" (play as Millie)
*Weeks ago.* Play Millie walking willingly into Saint-Cloud's intake — choosing
capture to plant her exploit at the core, knowing what it will cost her body. She
folds and leaves the second-to-last crane on the threshold.

### Cliffhanger
James reaches the processing core a breath too late: the cycle has started, and
Millie is inside it. Every screen lights with a calm, kind voice — **Mother**:
*"You brought her home to me, James. Thank you. Now stand still and watch."* The core
seals. The lights go red. **Cut to black.** *The darkest hour.*

---

# EPISODE 5 — "Crown of Stars"
**Stratum:** The Apex · **Play:** James (with a final Millie flashback)
**Mechanics combined:** Climbing · Parkour · Shooting · Swimming (escape)

### Open (in-game)
There is one way to stop the cycle: the override at the top of the Apex, where Saint
sits under the only real sky in Verge City. James climbs.

### The level
The vertical finale — **climb** the spire's exterior and shafts, **parkour** the
collapsing floors, **shoot** through Saint-Cloud's guard. **Books** buys the way up
at the cost of his own life (the comm goes to static; James keeps climbing). At the
crown you confront **Saint** — and you free **Millie** from the core. Together you
finish what she started: her exploit plus the wall of evidence, broadcast to every
screen in the city at once. The lights of Verge City flicker — and **come up like
stars.** Then the wounded spire begins to flood and fall, and you **swim and run**
the two of you out together.

### Flashback — "Counting Stars" (brief, intercut, play as Millie)
The lullaby, paid off: Mara at the window, the two children counting the lit
windows of the Sink like a sky. *"I'll always be one of them."*

### Ending
Dawn, on the broken lip of the Apex, the city laid out below and — for the first
time in years — actually visible. Millie is alive: thinner, scarred, changed, but
*hers*. James finally stops trying to carry her. He just sits beside her. She presses
the **last crane** into his scarred hand and finishes the lullaby. He found her. And
he lets her be exactly who she chose to become.

**THE END.**

## How the story is delivered in-game

* **Subtitles** — ambient lines (thoughts, callouts) float at the lower third while
  you keep playing; they don't stop the action.
* **Comm pop-ups** — when Books / Millie / Cray / Saint speak over the channel, a
  framed dialogue card appears with the speaker's name and colour; advance with the
  **action key / button** (and the game soft-pauses for important exchanges).
* **Meetings** — Books (Ep1), Millie (Ep3 & Ep5), Cray (Ep4) and Saint (Ep5) appear
  as **characters in the level**; reaching them triggers a face-to-face exchange.
* **Trigger zones** — dialogue, callouts and tutorials fire from invisible zones as
  you cross them, so the story is paced by *movement*, not by menus.
* **Flashbacks** — each is a short, fully **playable** level where you are **Millie**,
  and where the motifs (cranes, the lullaby, the scar) are planted.
